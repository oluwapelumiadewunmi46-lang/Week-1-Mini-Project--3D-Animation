# Week 1 Mini Project – Bouncing Ball and the 12 Principles of Animation

## Introduction

This project analyzes how a bouncing ball demonstrates the 12 Principles of Animation. By observing the ball as it falls, hits the ground, and rebounds, we can see how animators use these principles to create realistic and appealing motion.

## 1. Squash and Stretch
When the ball hits the ground, it squashes. As it leaves the ground, it stretches. This makes the motion feel more realistic and energetic.

## 2. Anticipation
The ball squashes before rebounding upward, preparing the viewer for the next movement.

## 3. Staging
The ball is presented against a simple background so that the motion is easy to understand.

## 4. Straight Ahead Action and Pose-to-Pose
The animation can be created using key poses such as the top position, impact position, and rebound position.

## 5. Follow Through and Overlapping Action
The squash and stretch continue briefly after impact, adding natural motion.

## 6. Slow In and Slow Out
The ball slows near the top of its arc and speeds up as it falls due to gravity.

## 7. Arcs
The ball follows curved paths during its movement, creating smooth and natural motion.

## 8. Secondary Action
The squash and stretch support the primary bouncing action.

## 9. Timing
The speed of the bounce communicates the ball's weight and energy loss.

## 10. Exaggeration
The squash and stretch may be exaggerated to make the bounce more entertaining.

## 11. Solid Drawing (Solid Posing)
The ball maintains its volume while changing shape during squash and stretch.

## 12. Appeal
The smooth movement and clear action make the animation visually appealing.

## Conclusion

A bouncing ball is a simple but powerful exercise that demonstrates all 12 Principles of Animation. Understanding these principles provides a foundation for creating more complex animations.
